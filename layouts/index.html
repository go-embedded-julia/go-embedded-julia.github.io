Julia, embedded in Go.
An interpreter that runs Julia's own Base.

go-embedded-julia is in development, and its parser and its lowering are released: v0.3.0 completes milestones M0 (the oracle harness, against julia 1.12.6), M1 (a Go port of JuliaSyntax), identical to julia's Meta.parseall on 1191 of 1191 files of julia's own tree, and M2 (lowering: a port of julia 1.12.6's femtolisp front end), identical to Meta.lower on 53 997 of 53 997 forms of that tree. Milestone M3 (the Core runtime) is in progress: types, subtyping and specificity are done (core, v0.4.0); dispatch is next. It will be a pure-Go, embeddable Julia interpreter: julia's parser and lowering ported to Go, and an interpreter over the lowered IR that runs Julia's Base unchanged, for the julia cells of go-notebook and its WebAssembly playground.

An interpreter, and honest about it.

It keeps Julia's semantics, not its native speed. An interpreter cannot match Julia's JIT on loops, so heavy computation goes through the Go libraries (go-ndarray, go-bignum, go-fft). The comparisons that can hold are start-up and first result, and the kernels routed to those libraries. The plan lists every interpreter speed statement as unverified, except the parser's: on one core, parsing julia 1.12.6's share/julia tree takes 0.98 s against 1.39 s for Meta.parseall (0.70x). No speed is claimed for the lowering.
